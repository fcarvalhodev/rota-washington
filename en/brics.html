Parallel 3 · Payments and geopolitics

BRICS, the dollar and Pix

Pix is instant and uses infrastructure operated by the Central Bank, with the participation of banks and other providers. It is generally free for individuals, with exceptions; businesses can be charged. This took room away from American card networks and put a Brazilian payment system in the middle of the dispute between the US and BRICS over the future of the dollar.

How Pix entered the dispute

Nov 2024Still president-elect, Trump threatens a 100% tariff against BRICS

The condition: the bloc may not create a currency or mechanism to replace the dollar in trade.

Jul 6–7, 2025BRICS summit in Rio and threat of an extra 10% tariff

It applies to any country aligned with the bloc's “anti-American” policies. Lula says he gave it “no importance”.

Jul 9, 2025Trump's letter imposes a 50% tariff on Brazil

The text cites Jair Bolsonaro's trial and rulings against American platforms.

Jul 15, 2025Section 301 includes electronic payments

It is the first time Pix officially appears as a trade problem for the US.

Oct 16, 2025Demand no. 4: the Central Bank's “competitive neutrality”

The Central Bank both regulates and operates Pix. The US asks that it not favor its own system.

Why BRICS enters this story

Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa and new members discuss ways of trading among themselves without going through the dollar. No single bloc-wide system is in operation today. For Washington, the risk is the dollar losing the role that gives the US the power to impose financial sanctions. A Pix linked to systems of other bloc countries would be one piece of that puzzle. That is what Flávio's proposal on “non-Western” systems aims to close off.

Who proposes what

Lula government

Treats Pix as public infrastructure and says it is not up for negotiation. Calls the new surcharge unjustified.

Flávio

Promises to keep Pix free. Offered the US a law that would prevent Pix from connecting to “non-Western” systems and asked to postpone the tariff until after the election.

Eduardo

Suggested replacing Pix with Zelle, an American system.

Flávio did not endorse the proposal.

Two readings

Those who see a threat to sovereignty

  • Deciding what Pix can connect to is a decision for Brazil's Central Bank, not for an agreement with the US.
  • Pix lowered costs for stores and consumers. Pressure against it protects foreign companies' profits.
  • Giving up alternatives to the dollar leaves the country more exposed to US sanctions, like the one applied to Moraes.

Those who see pragmatism

  • The US is Brazil's second-largest trading partner. Giving ground on technical points can avoid tariffs that cost jobs.
  • Keeping Pix away from Russian or Chinese systems keeps Brazil from becoming a target of secondary sanctions.
  • Flávio's proposal keeps Pix free in Brazil.

Sources and limits of this reading

Whether Pix is free depends on the type of user and transaction. The design simplifies infrastructure and does not eliminate providers' participation. The USTR's investigation and tariff action have different phases, scopes and acts.
