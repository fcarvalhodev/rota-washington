Parallel 5 · Critical minerals

Rare earths

They are 17 elements used in magnets for electric cars, wind turbines, phones and weapons. China dominates production and refining, the US wants an alternative, and Brazil has the second-largest known reserve. That is why the topic appears in the American demands, in Flávio's and Lula's meetings and in the sale of a mine in Goiás.

Where the power lies

Having the reserve is only the start. The value lies in the next stages, and the largest of them is dominated by China.

Two readings

Those who see a threat to sovereignty

  • The only producing mine is already American, and part of what it produces has a destination set by another government.
  • Giving the US advance notice and preference over mines takes from Brazil the power to negotiate with whoever pays more or offers more technology.
  • Exporting unrefined ore repeats the model of selling cheap raw materials and buying expensive products.

Those who see an opportunity

  • American capital gets projects off the ground faster than Brazil would on its own.
  • An alternative to China is of interest to the world and can give Brazil bargaining power.
  • Partnerships can bring refining and magnet factories, if technology transfer is required by contract.

Sources and limits of this reading

Mineral reserves are not the same as immediate production or refining capacity. The US$565 million is a loan; the acquisition announcement in April and the closing in September are distinct events.
