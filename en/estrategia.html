Trump
Corollary

The document that guides US foreign policy put the Americas at the top of its priorities and revived the Monroe Doctrine with a new addition. This page shows what it says, where it comes from and how it already shows up in decisions about Brazil.

“American dominance in the Western Hemisphere will never be questioned again.”Donald Trump, quoted in a State Department video, Aug 11, 2026

200 years of doctrine

The Monroe Doctrine changed meaning over time. The Trump Corollary takes up its most interventionist version.

1823Monroe Doctrine

James Monroe warns the European powers that he will not accept new colonies in the Americas.

1904Roosevelt Corollary

The US claims the right to intervene in neighboring countries. Occupations in the Caribbean and Central America follow.

1933Good Neighbor

Franklin Roosevelt renounces armed intervention in the region.

2013“The era is over”

Secretary of State John Kerry declares at the OAS that the era of the Monroe Doctrine has ended.

2025Trump Corollary

The doctrine returns as a central pillar, now aimed at China and the cartels.

What the document says

The five pillars of the strategy for the Americas, according to coverage of the official text.

Priority number one

The Americas move ahead of Europe, the Middle East and Asia on the list of regional priorities, treated as the first line of defense of US territory.

Deny competitors

Prevent powers from outside the hemisphere from positioning forces or “threatening capabilities” in the region.

“We will deny non-Hemispheric competitors the ability to position forces.”

Strategic assets

Block rivals' control of ports, energy and telecommunications. According to JURIST, the text conditions aid and investment on unwinding projects financed by China.

Recruit and expand allies

Work with “established friends” against migration and drugs and extend that network to new partners.

Force against cartels

Strikes on vessels suspected of carrying drugs, treated as “narco-terrorist” targets, in the language of armed conflict.

And where does Brazil fit?

What is at stake for the country, and the two readings competing in the debate.

Those who see a threat to sovereignty

  • The strategy treats the region as an exclusive sphere of influence and uses tariffs, visas and sanctions to obtain decisions that belong to Brazil.
  • Designating criminal factions as terrorists has criminal, immigration and financial effects. It is not automatic authorization to use military force on Brazilian territory; that would require its own basis and conditions.
  • Limiting China, Brazil's largest trading partner, imposes an economic cost decided in Washington.

Those who see an opportunity

  • Aligning with the US could bring investment in critical minerals and access to technology.
  • Cooperation against criminal factions could help fight organized crime.
  • Reducing dependence on China would, in this view, also be a sovereign decision.

Sources and limits of this reading

This page interprets a government strategy. Stated objectives do not prove all the effects or motives of each action. Terrorism designations and authorization for military force are different legal instruments.
