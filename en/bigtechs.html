Parallel 4 · Platforms and courts

Big tech and the courts

Since 2024, Brazil's Supreme Court has ordered American platforms to comply with Brazilian orders, and the Trump administration treats those orders as censorship. This fight became a reason for tariffs, for sanctions and for five of the 21 demands. This page separates what happened from what is suspicion.

Meta: what changed on October 10–11

Checked on 10/12/2026, Tokyo time. The events below are dated in Brazil. There is no conclusion of deliberate interference in the sources consulted.

The TSE asked for explanations.

According to Agência Brasil, Justice Bellizze gave 48 hours for explanations about reach, recommendations, blocks and ads. It is a measure to investigate, not a ruling that Meta favored Flávio.

What does the campaign allege?

Selective treatment of pro-Lula content and of profiles from his political camp. Meta's statement denies bias and attributes some of the episodes to technical problems that also affected other accounts and countries.

Allegation and response
Is there evidence of failures?

In an earlier experiment, Amnesty submitted 15 ads, 14 with disinformation, and reports that eight misleading ads were approved. They were cancelled before going live. The test points to moderation failures; it did not measure favoritism toward Lula or Flávio.

What information could clear up the suspicion?

The order, according to Metrópoles, requires preserving records and explaining measures by profile, human or automated decisions, the basis for blocking ads and metric anomalies. It also asks for information on synthetic profiles and the treatment of both candidacies.

In this page's reading, a drop in reach, follower numbers or a reversed block do not, on their own, identify political intent. That requires comparing accounts, periods, paid advertising, rules applied and technical records. The company's denial does not replace that verification either.

Status of the inquiry: as of this check, we have not found the full technical response to the order or a final ruling on selectivity. The announced deadline runs from notification; we do not treat the date of the news as proof that it has expired.

Accounts taken down by Meta itself, such as those of Jones Manoel and Chavoso da USP, the comparison with right-wing accounts and Meta's defense at the TSE are on the Algorithm Route.

The WhatsApp channel is a separate episode

On 10/11, Nunes Marques suspended the “Exército do Lula” channel at the request of Flávio's campaign, and inspector general Cueva revoked the order the same day, according to UOL. On 10/13, according to Poder360, Nunes Marques annulled Cueva's decision and made the channel's return conditional on registering its address with the Electoral Courts. Meta began monitoring the channel and, under the same criteria, public channels linked to Flávio's campaign. Here the restriction comes from a court decision; it is not evidence of an autonomous choice by Meta.

Fact or suspicion?

Claims that circulate on the subject, with the level of proof for each.

Zuckerberg aligned himself with Trump against Latin American courts.

His public statement in January 2025.

True
American platforms disobeyed Brazilian court orders.

X and Rumble were suspended for not complying with rulings and having no legal representative in the country.

True
Meta harmed left-wing profiles near the election.

Restrictions were reported and reversed. The TSE asked for technical data on 10/10; the alleged selective treatment is still under investigation. Meta denies bias.

Under investigation
The Supreme Court censors American companies.

The Supreme Court says it applies Brazilian law to those operating in Brazil. The Trump administration calls this censorship. It is a dispute of interpretation.

Disputed
Meta acted on the Trump administration's orders in the election.

The sources consulted present no proof of orders from the Trump administration. A political relationship and suspicion of selectivity do not demonstrate that chain of command.

No proof

Two readings

Judicial sovereignty

  • Companies that profit in Brazil must follow the law and the rulings of Brazilian courts, as they follow Europe's.
  • Using tariffs and sanctions to change court rulings is interference in another branch of another country.
  • The demands would create special rules for American companies only.

Freedom of expression

  • Sealed orders to remove profiles make defense and public scrutiny of the rulings harder.
  • The new reading of Article 19 may lead platforms to remove too much content for fear of punishment.
  • Asking for prior notice and a right of appeal is a principle of due process.

Sources and limits of this reading

Meta's January 2025 announcement refers initially to the US. Instances of blocking and error must be distinguished from proof of systematic political orientation or of a government order.
