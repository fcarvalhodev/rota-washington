The 21 demands

According to the reports cited, a US draft for negotiating the tariff hike contained a list that goes far beyond trade: elections, court rulings, the Central Bank, big tech and strategic minerals. Below, each item with the argument of its defenders, that of its critics, the level of risk to sovereignty and whether it coincides with proposals by Flávio Bolsonaro or allies.

What happened to the list

The US delivers the draft with 21 items.

Brazil sends a counterproposal to end the tariffs.

Jan 2026

A new draft, with 14 points, is also rejected.

Sep 17, 2026

The list becomes public. Lula calls it a “dead file”. Trade negotiations continue.

How we read the risk

High: the item limits decisions of a Brazilian institution (the Judiciary, the Central Bank, the Electoral Courts), makes foreign law apply in Brazil or gives another country preference over strategic assets.

Medium: the item reduces the room for Congress or regulators to create rules and taxes in the future, or requires geopolitical alignment.

Low: a commercial or environmental term common in agreements between countries.

This classification is an editorial reading by this page, not by an official body. The coincidence with Flávio follows ICL Notícias's survey, which counted 16 items. Marked here are the 14 whose cross-check the report details.

Sources and limits of this reading

The items are reproduced from reports on a draft. There was no independent check of the full document in this review. Risk and political coincidence are editorial classifications; the items are not presented as an agreement in force or an official classification.
